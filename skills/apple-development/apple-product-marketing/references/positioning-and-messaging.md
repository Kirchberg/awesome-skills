# Positioning and messaging

Use this reference before writing tactics. Position the product around a
specific customer context and provable value, then adapt that message to each
surface.

## Contents

- Positioning sequence
- Audience and jobs
- Message hierarchy
- Claims and proof
- Voice and tone
- Quality checks

## Positioning sequence

Work in this order:

1. Identify the best-fit customers or users and the situation that makes them
   receptive now.
2. Name the job they are trying to complete and the desired outcome.
3. List the alternatives they would use if the product did not exist,
   including doing nothing, manual workflows, and indirect competitors.
4. Identify capabilities that are both different from those alternatives and
   relevant to the job.
5. Translate each differentiated capability into customer value.
6. Select the market category or frame that makes that value understandable.
7. Record credible proof: product behavior, demonstration, research, review
   theme, measured result, certification, or other approved evidence.
8. Write a positioning statement for internal alignment, not as automatic
   customer-facing copy.

Use this internal form:

```text
For [best-fit audience] who need to [job in context],
[product] is a [market frame] that [primary value].
Unlike [current alternatives], it [differentiated capability],
supported by [proof].
```

Do not fill missing proof with adjectives, market leadership, user counts, or
performance claims that have not been verified.

## Audience and jobs

Describe audience segments with behavior and context:

- triggering situation;
- current workflow or alternative;
- desired progress;
- anxiety, switching cost, or constraint;
- product literacy and lifecycle state;
- search or channel intent;
- device, storefront, locale, and accessibility context when relevant.

Avoid relying on demographic personas when they do not explain choice. Separate
new prospects, evaluators, active users, lapsed users, subscribers considering
cancellation, and reviewers because they need different evidence and calls to
action.

## Message hierarchy

Build one hierarchy per material intent segment:

1. **Promise**: the clearest user outcome.
2. **Primary benefit**: why the outcome matters in the current situation.
3. **Supporting benefits**: two or three distinct reasons to care.
4. **Proof**: observable product behavior or approved evidence.
5. **Feature bridge**: the capability that creates each benefit.
6. **Objection response**: concise evidence for the main uncertainty.
7. **CTA**: the next action that matches the user's readiness.

Map the hierarchy to channel capacity. A subtitle or first screenshot may carry
only promise and benefit; a description or website can add proof and detail.
Do not force every feature into every surface.

## Claims and proof

Maintain a claim ledger containing:

- exact claim;
- audience and surface;
- product or research evidence;
- owner and approval status;
- locale limitations;
- expiration or revalidation date;
- prohibited or safer alternative wording.

Use concrete verbs and user outcomes. Prefer “organize receipts from Photos”
over “revolutionary intelligent productivity.” Qualify comparisons, savings,
health, financial, safety, privacy, accessibility, or environmental claims and
escalate them for domain or legal review where appropriate.

Keep future capabilities out of current acquisition promises unless the
surface and program explicitly support pre-order or upcoming messaging and the
status is unmistakable.

## Voice and tone

Keep voice stable and adapt tone to the user's state. Define the voice with a
small number of usable traits, each with “do” and “do not” examples. Tune tone
along these dimensions:

- formal to casual;
- restrained to enthusiastic;
- direct to reassuring;
- serious to playful.

Use lower enthusiasm and higher clarity for errors, incidents, billing,
privacy, safety, and cancellation. Use concise confidence for onboarding and
task completion. Let launch copy be energetic only when it remains specific and
truthful.

Create a channel brief before drafting:

```text
Goal
Audience
User state
Channel and constraints
Core message
Evidence
CTA
Voice traits
Tone for this moment
Required facts
Forbidden or unverified claims
Metric
```

## Quality checks

Check every customer-facing line for:

- **clarity**: use familiar, unambiguous language;
- **concision**: remove filler, repetition, and decorative claims;
- **character**: preserve an intentional voice without hiding the meaning;
- **specificity**: connect the promise to a real capability;
- **continuity**: keep the same intent from discovery to destination;
- **accessibility**: avoid relying on visual position, color, idiom, or
  unexplained jargon;
- **translatability**: use stable terms, explicit references, and complete
  thoughts;
- **evidence**: support every material claim.

Read copy aloud, inspect it in its real UI or creative context, and test
truncation and comprehension with representative users when the decision
matters.
