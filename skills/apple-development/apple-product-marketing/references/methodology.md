# Marketing methodology

Use this reference to frame broad work, control evidence quality, and avoid
premature tactics.

## Contents

- Source hierarchy
- Intake and scope
- Evidence ledger
- End-to-end workflow
- Diagnosis workflow
- Decision and completion gates

## Source hierarchy

Apply sources in this order:

1. Current Apple policy, App Store Connect Help, Apple Ads Help, Human
   Interface Guidelines, and API documentation for platform rules.
2. Current first-party product evidence: the shipped build, approved roadmap,
   metadata, creatives, research, analytics definitions, and experiment data.
3. Customer evidence: interviews, surveys, support themes, review mining,
   search terms, and observed behavior.
4. Established positioning, content-design, localization, and measurement
   frameworks for methods.
5. Vendor research and benchmarks for hypothesis generation only.

Resolve conflicts in favor of the higher source. If Apple documentation and
current App Store Connect behavior appear to conflict, report both, capture the
date and account or storefront context, and avoid asserting a universal rule.

Verify live documentation before quoting:

- metadata limits or editable fields;
- supported regions, storefronts, roles, or device types;
- OS requirements and feature availability;
- submission and review behavior;
- analytics definitions, attribution windows, and privacy behavior;
- announced or seasonal App Store surfaces.

## Intake and scope

Collect only what changes the decision:

- product, platform, category, lifecycle stage, and business model;
- shipped capabilities and approved near-term capabilities;
- audience, user state, job, current alternatives, and entry context;
- storefronts, locales, localization status, and supported OS versions;
- objective, baseline, horizon, budget, owner, and approval process;
- current product page, Custom Product Pages, events, promoted purchases,
  campaigns, website, and lifecycle channels;
- acquisition, conversion, activation, retention, proceeds, and quality data;
- privacy, regulated-claim, trademark, accessibility, and brand constraints.

For an audit, request the actual metadata, creative order, storefront and device
context, not a prose summary alone. For a diagnosis, request the metric
definition, numerator, denominator, cohort, comparison window, traffic source,
and known product or campaign changes.

Do not block on low-impact missing details. State a reversible assumption and
show what evidence would reverse the recommendation.

## Evidence ledger

Maintain five labels:

- **Verified fact**: supported by a current primary source or inspected product
  evidence.
- **User-provided claim**: accepted as project context but not independently
  verified.
- **Assumption**: needed to proceed and explicitly exposed.
- **Hypothesis**: causal or predictive statement designed to be tested.
- **Unknown**: a gap that limits confidence or execution.

Attach an owner and validation method to material assumptions and unknowns.
Never upgrade an assumption to a fact because it is repeated in a vendor blog
or competitor teardown.

## End-to-end workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** State the objective, audience, surface, deadline,
   available levers, and decision that the work must enable.
2. **Establish positioning.** Identify the best-fit audience, job, alternatives,
   differentiated capabilities, value, proof, and market frame.
3. **Build intent segments.** Group by problem, use case, feature, category,
   brand, competitor, lifecycle state, or seasonal context when relevant.
4. **Design message continuity.** Map query or channel to promise, creative,
   product page, CTA, onboarding state, and deep-linked destination.
5. **Choose the surface portfolio.** Use only surfaces that fit audience state:
   search, browse, custom pages, events, featuring, ads, email, social, review
   responses, website, or another approved channel.
6. **Create and localize.** Draft from verified capabilities, then transcreate
   and validate by storefront. Keep a glossary and claim ledger.
7. **Check policy and readiness.** Review metadata accuracy, creative truth,
   rights, accessibility, privacy, instrumentation, owners, and launch timing.
8. **Measure and learn.** Use a predeclared primary outcome, quality guardrails,
   cohorts, decision rule, and follow-up action.

## Diagnosis workflow

Locate the break before recommending work:

1. **Visibility**: Are relevant people seeing the app in search, browse,
   editorial, ads, referrals, or web discovery?
2. **Page engagement**: Do product page views follow impressions for the
   intended surface and intent?
3. **Download conversion**: Do first-time downloads follow page views, using
   the exact App Store Connect definition and cohort?
4. **Activation**: Do acquired users reach the product's first value?
5. **Retention and engagement**: Do they continue using the promised value?
6. **Monetization and quality**: Do proceeds, refunds, crashes, support burden,
   and review sentiment remain acceptable?

Segment by storefront, locale, device, source, campaign, product page, app
version, and new versus returning user only when the data supports that grain.
Check instrumentation and mix shifts before assigning causality to creative or
metadata.

## Decision and completion gates

Require these before calling a plan ready:

- a verified product promise and intended audience;
- a named owner for every execution surface;
- approved source copy and locale-specific QA;
- current platform and policy checks;
- instrumentation available before traffic starts;
- a primary metric, guardrails, cohort, window, and decision rule;
- launch, pause, rollback, and follow-up criteria.

Report confidence and remaining uncertainty. A well-structured hypothesis
backlog is a valid outcome; invented certainty is not.
