# Launch and communications

Use this reference for a new-app launch, major update, market expansion,
feature announcement, seasonal moment, lifecycle message, incident update, or
review response.

## Contents

- Classify the launch
- Build the launch plan
- Prepare App Store opportunities
- Write channel-specific copy
- Handle operational and review messages
- Measure and learn

## Classify the launch

Choose the actual event:

- new app or game;
- major version or feature update;
- new market or localization;
- content, season, live event, or promoted purchase;
- pre-order for an eligible unreleased app;
- re-engagement or win-back moment;
- maintenance, degradation, incident, or recovery.

Do not recommend pre-order as a mechanism for an ordinary update to an existing
app. Do not force an In-App Event or featuring nomination onto a release that
does not meet current program purpose or eligibility.

## Build the launch plan

Define:

- launch objective and decision horizon;
- audience segments and user states;
- product truth, proof, exclusions, and readiness dependencies;
- core message and per-segment message hierarchy;
- product page and Custom Product Page destinations;
- channels, owners, assets, review lead times, and publication sequence;
- localization and storefront plan;
- analytics, campaign links, attribution, guardrails, and reporting cadence;
- support, incident, pause, rollback, and follow-up plans.

Work backward from the release and current App Store review or editorial lead
times. Use relative phases instead of pretending one calendar fits every app:

1. **Foundation**: positioning, product proof, audience, instrumentation,
   policy, rights, and locale scope.
2. **Production**: metadata, creative, pages, events, ads, website, email,
   social, support macros, and QA.
3. **Submission and rehearsal**: App Review, nomination, link and deep-link
   tests, preview, analytics verification, approvals, and dry run.
4. **Launch window**: coordinated publication, monitoring, support, and
   controlled response.
5. **Learning window**: cohort-quality review, experiment decisions,
   evergreen updates, and retrospective.

## Prepare App Store opportunities

- Use a pre-order only for a currently eligible new app and verify current
  timing, territories, pricing, build, and publication rules.
- Submit a Featuring Nomination around a genuine story and current eligibility.
  Follow current Apple timing guidance, include complete assets and context,
  and never imply guaranteed selection.
- Use an In-App Event for a time-bound, eligible experience with accurate
  metadata, media, dates, audience, and deep link.
- Use Custom Product Pages for distinct intent or audience promises and keep
  each referring channel aligned.
- Use promoted in-app purchases when the item itself is a discovery-relevant
  entry point and its availability and destination are ready.
- Use campaign links or approved channel parameters before traffic begins.

Treat seasonal or announced App Store capabilities as unavailable until
verified live for the relevant account and storefront.

## Write channel-specific copy

Create the channel brief from
`Goal → Audience → User state → Channel → Core message → Evidence → CTA → Tone`.

Then adapt:

- **Release notes**: state user-visible value and important behavior plainly;
  avoid an internal ticket list or vague “bug fixes” when specifics are safe.
- **Promotional text**: lead with current value or timely news; keep it
  consistent with the product page and current field rules.
- **Email**: align sender, subject, preview text, body hierarchy, CTA,
  preferences, and destination.
- **Social**: make each post complete without clickbait; adapt format and tone
  to the platform while preserving the core claim.
- **In-app message or notification**: use only when timely, relevant, and
  permission-compatible; make dismissal and destination behavior clear.
- **Product Hunt or community launch**: participate according to current
  platform rules, disclose relationships, and avoid artificial engagement.

Do not duplicate the same copy across channels. Preserve the same promise while
adapting length, proof, timing, and CTA.

## Handle operational and review messages

For maintenance or incidents:

1. Lead with user impact and current status.
2. State affected functions and scope only when verified.
3. Give a safe workaround if one exists.
4. State the next update time or resolution criterion.
5. Avoid blame, speculation, false precision, and promotional tone.
6. Close with resolution, any required user action, and follow-up.

For review responses, protect personal data, acknowledge the experience, give
verified help, move account-specific work to private support, and never ask the
reviewer to raise a rating. Route repeated themes to product and marketing
owners.

## Measure and learn

Give every channel:

- one primary objective;
- a tagged or otherwise attributable destination where permitted;
- delivery, engagement, destination, activation, retention, and quality
  measures appropriate to the channel;
- a comparison baseline or test;
- an owner and review time;
- a next action for strong, weak, harmful, or inconclusive results.

Do not sum incomparable attribution systems into a fictional total. Report
overlap, privacy thresholds, missing data, and channel-specific definitions.
